CSS: Gradients

Linear Gradients

linear-gradient(to right, lightyellow, lightblue) — A linear gradient transitions colors along a straight line. The gradient starts with light yellow on the left and fades to light blue on the right. The direction to right means the color axis runs horizontally from left to right.

linear-gradient(to bottom, lightyellow, lightblue) — A linear gradient transitions colors along a straight line. The gradient starts with light yellow at the top and fades to light blue at the bottom. The direction to bottom means the color axis runs vertically from top to bottom.

Radial Gradients

radial-gradient(circle, green, yellow) — A radial gradient radiates colors outward from a central point. The shape circle ensures the gradient spreads evenly in all directions as a perfect circle. It starts with green at the center and fades to yellow at the edges.

radial-gradient(ellipse, green, yellow) — A radial gradient radiates colors outward from a central point. The shape ellipse stretches the gradient to follow the proportions of the element, forming an oval. It starts with green at the center and fades to yellow at the edges.

Conic Gradients

conic-gradient(lightcoral, lightyellow, lightgreen, lightskyblue, lightcoral) — A conic gradient rotates colors around a center point, like a color wheel. Unlike radial gradients, the transition happens angularly rather than radially. This example cycles through light coral → light yellow → light green → light sky blue and back to light coral, completing a full 360° rotation.

conic-gradient(from 45deg, lightyellow, lightblue) — A conic gradient rotates colors around a center point. The keyword from 45deg sets the starting angle of the rotation, shifting the start of the gradient by 45°. It transitions from light yellow to light blue going clockwise around the center.
